# Drills: mixed practice

The workbooks go domain by domain, which is the right way to *learn* the material. It's the wrong way to *test* yourself on it. When every question in a session is about Domain 3, you already know the answer lives in Domain 3. Half the work is done before you've read the scenario.

The real exam doesn't do that. Question 14 is about tokens, question 15 is about CloudTrail, question 16 is a fine-tuning trap. **Figuring out which bucket a question belongs to is half the skill**, and blocked practice never trains it. Mixed practice (interleaving, if you want the learning-science name) feels harder and slower. That's the point. It holds up a lot better on exam day than the stuff that felt smooth.

So: drills. Twenty original scenario questions each, every domain shuffled together, written in the exam's style and built only from what's in the topic notes. These aren't real exam questions, and they're not modeled on dumps. If a question here teaches you something the notes don't cover, that's a bug.

## How I run one

1. **Twenty questions, about 30 minutes.** That's roughly 1.5 minutes each, which is close to exam pace. Set a timer.
2. **Closed book.** No notes, no tabs, no playground.
3. **Answer all twenty before opening a single answer.** Write your letters down first. Peeking after question 3 turns the rest of the drill into reading.
4. **Grade at the end.** Open each answer block, mark right or wrong, and fill in the score line at the bottom.
5. **Log every miss.** Three fields: the **topic**, **what I picked**, and **why it was wrong**. "Misread it" is a valid reason, but be honest about whether it was the reading or the knowledge.
6. **Under 16/20?** Re-read the linked topic notes for the misses, then redo *just the misses* three days later.

Each answer gives the right letter, why it's right, why the most tempting wrong option is wrong, and a link to the topic note it came from. The "why not" line matters most. Distractors on these exams are built from real misconceptions, and knowing why the wrong answer looked right is how you stop picking it.

## Schedule

- **One drill a week** while you're studying a cert. Do it on the same day each week so it actually happens.
- **AIF phase:** alternate AIF-01 and AIF-02. By the second time through, your score should be well clear of 16.
- **MLA phase and beyond:** keep the cert drills going, and start mixing in [CROSS-01](cross-01.md) every few weeks. It interleaves all three certs at their own levels, which keeps AIF from fading while you're deep in pipelines.
- **The week before the real exam:** one fresh drill at full exam conditions, then nothing new. Just review the miss log.

A drill isn't a practice exam. The official practice exam is still the gate (consistently ≥80% before booking). Drills are the weekly reps that get you there.
